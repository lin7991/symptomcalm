Post-COVID Fatigue: Rebuilding Your Energy When Long COVID Won't Let Go

Symptom Guide · 9 min read

You had COVID months ago. Your test turned negative. Your fever broke. Your cough mostly cleared. By every conventional measure, you recovered.

So why does your body still feel like it's running on fumes?

Maybe you can manage a few hours of work before your brain turns to fog. Maybe a short walk leaves you breathless, or your muscles ache in ways they never did before. Maybe you wake up exhausted after nine hours of sleep — and by mid-afternoon you're staring at your screen, unable to string a sentence together.

This is post-COVID fatigue, one of the most common and stubborn symptoms of Long COVID. It's not laziness, deconditioning, or depression — though it can certainly feel like all three. It's a real physiological state, and it affects an estimated 10-20% of people who contract COVID-19, according to research published in the Journal of Infectious Diseases.

Traditional Chinese Medicine offers a structured framework for understanding why post-COVID fatigue persists — and what you can do to support your body's recovery, step by step.

For a broader overview of post-illness fatigue in general, see our guide to post-illness fatigue and Qi deficiency. This article focuses specifically on the patterns seen after COVID-19.

Why COVID Is Different From Other Illnesses

Post-COVID fatigue is not the same as the exhaustion you feel after a bad flu or a chest infection. Research has identified several features that make COVID unique in how it affects the body's energy systems:

  • Multi-system involvement — COVID affects the respiratory, cardiovascular, neurological, and immune systems simultaneously. In TCM terms, multiple organ systems are drained at once, not just one.
  • Persistent inflammation — Studies have shown that some people with Long COVID continue to show elevated inflammatory markers months after the acute infection. In TCM theory, this resembles a residual pathogenic factor that the body hasn't fully cleared.
  • Micro-clotting and endothelial damage — Research suggests that COVID may cause microscopic blood clotting that impairs oxygen delivery to tissues. This aligns with the TCM concept of Blood Stasis — where circulation is compromised at a microvascular level.
  • Mitochondrial dysfunction — Some researchers hypothesize that COVID may impair mitochondrial function, reducing the cells' ability to produce energy. In TCM terms, this is a profound depletion of the body's Qi-producing capacity.
The key insight: In TCM theory, a pathogen that penetrates deeper than a surface-level cold or flu creates a more complex recovery pattern. Post-COVID fatigue often involves three interconnected patterns — Lung Qi deficiency, Dampness-Phlegm retention, and Kidney Qi depletion — and recovery requires addressing all three.

Pattern 1: Lung Qi Deficiency with Residual Phlegm

The Lungs are the first organ system affected by COVID. In TCM theory, the Lungs govern Qi and respiration — they're like the body's engine intake system, drawing in air and transforming it into usable energy. After a significant respiratory infection, Lung Qi is often depleted, and the engine struggles to produce enough power.

Lung Qi Deficiency (肺气虚) — The Depleted Engine

You might have this if: Your fatigue is paired with shortness of breath, a weak or quiet voice, a lingering dry or phlegmy cough, and a tendency to sigh frequently. You may feel like you simply can't get enough air — even when a pulse oximeter says your oxygen levels are normal. Sweating easily with minimal exertion is another common sign.

The TCM view: During a COVID infection, the Lungs mount an intense immune response to expel the pathogen. This consumes enormous amounts of Lung Qi. After the acute phase, the Lungs are left in a depleted state — like a fire department that just fought a five-alarm blaze and now lacks the fuel for its trucks.

Why it persists: Unlike a common cold, COVID often leaves behind residual Phlegm-Heat or Phlegm-Dampness that continues to block the Lung channels. The Lungs can't fully replenish their Qi because the airway is partially obstructed by lingering congestion at the channel level.

Signs of residual Phlegm after COVID: A cough that produces pale or white phlegm (Phlegm-Dampness) or yellow/sticky phlegm (Phlegm-Heat). A sensation of something stuck in the throat that you can't clear. Phlegm that's hardest to clear in the morning or after lying down.

Pattern 2: Spleen Qi Deficiency with Dampness

In TCM theory, the Spleen is your body's power plant — it transforms food into Qi and Blood, the fuel every organ needs to function. During a COVID infection, the body diverts energy away from digestion to fight the pathogen, leaving the Spleen weakened. After the infection passes, the Spleen is often too depleted to efficiently convert food into usable energy.

Spleen Qi Deficiency with Dampness (脾虚湿困) — The Waterlogged Power Plant

You might have this if: Your fatigue worsens after eating — especially heavy, greasy, or sweet foods. You feel heavy, sluggish, and mentally foggy after meals. Your digestion may be slow, with bloating, loose stools, or a sensation of fullness that lingers for hours. You may also notice that your body feels "heavy" or that your limbs feel like they're moving through water.

The TCM view: When the Spleen is weak, it can't transform and transport fluids efficiently. Fluids accumulate and become Dampness — a heavy, turbid substance that weighs the body down. This is why post-COVID fatigue often feels like a physical weight, not just tiredness. The Dampness sits in the tissues, making every movement feel effortful.

Why it's common after COVID: COVID frequently causes gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, loss of appetite, diarrhea) in addition to respiratory symptoms. This directly impacts the Spleen during the acute phase, and recovery requires rebuilding Spleen function alongside the respiratory system.

Dampness vs simple fatigue: Simple Qi deficiency feels like "low battery" — you run out of energy quickly. Dampness feels like "moving through mud" — your body is heavy, your head feels wrapped in cotton, and rest alone doesn't fully clear it.

Pattern 3: Kidney Qi Depletion

This is the pattern that distinguishes post-COVID fatigue from simple post-cold fatigue. In TCM theory, a deep or prolonged illness can reach the Kidney system — the body's deepest energy reserve.

Kidney Qi Depletion (肾气虚) — The Depleted Battery Bank

You might have this if: Your fatigue includes lower back ache or weakness, poor short-term memory, brain fog, tinnitus or ringing in the ears, and feeling profoundly drained in a way that sleep doesn't fix. You may also notice that your hair feels thinner or your joints feel weaker. Libido may be noticeably lower.

The TCM view: The Kidneys store Jing (Essence) — your body's deepest vitality reserve, like a battery bank that powers everything else. When a prolonged illness like COVID pushes the body to its limits, it draws on Kidney Jing to sustain the immune response. This creates a state of profound depletion that takes the longest to recover from.

Why it's a hallmark of Long COVID: Unlike a cold that resolves in days, COVID often has a prolonged course with lingering symptoms that last weeks or months. This sustained demand on the body's resources is what depletes the Kidney. Patients with this pattern often describe feeling "older" or "depleted" in a way that's fundamentally different from ordinary tiredness.

Kidney deficiency vs Lung deficiency: Lung Qi deficiency feels like "running out of breath." Kidney Qi deficiency feels like "the battery won't hold a charge." Both patterns can coexist.

Pattern 4: Blood Stasis from Microvascular Changes

Emerging research has identified micro-clotting and endothelial dysfunction as features of Long COVID. In TCM theory, this is recognizable as a Blood Stasis pattern — circulation is compromised at the microvascular level, affecting oxygen and nutrient delivery to tissues.

Blood Stasis (血瘀) — Congested Microcirculation

You might have this if: Your fatigue includes sharp, stabbing, or fixed pains in specific locations — chest, muscles, joints — that don't shift or improve with movement. Your complexion may appear darkened or dull. You may notice varicose veins, spider veins, or easy bruising. Some people report a sensation of "pins and needles" in the extremities.

The TCM view: Blood Stasis is like silt accumulating in a slow-moving river — the flow is still happening at the macro level, but micro-circulation is compromised. This means oxygen and nutrients aren't reaching tissues efficiently, which directly contributes to fatigue, poor recovery, and lingering pain.

Which Pattern Do You Have? A Self-Assessment Guide

Most people with post-COVID fatigue have a combination of these patterns. This simple guide can help you identify which pattern is dominant in your experience:

Dominant Feeling Likely Pattern
Short of breath, weak voice, lingering cough Lung Qi Deficiency
Brain fog, heaviness, worse after eating Spleen Qi Deficiency with Dampness
Profound exhaustion, lower back ache, poor memory Kidney Qi Depletion
Fixed pain, cold extremities, dull complexion Blood Stasis

If you identify with one dominant pattern, focus your recovery efforts there first. If you see yourself in two or three, start with Lung and Spleen (the first to be affected) before addressing the deeper Kidney pattern.

5 TCM-Inspired Approaches to Support Post-COVID Recovery

These are not medical recommendations — they are traditionally used approaches in TCM theory. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider before making changes to your health routine, especially if you have other medical conditions or are taking medications.

1. Rebuild With Warming, Digestible Foods

When both Lung Qi and Spleen Qi are depleted, the digestibility of food matters as much as its nutritional content. In TCM theory, this means prioritizing foods that are easy to digest and warming to the system:

  • Congee (rice porridge) — This is the classic post-COVID recovery food in TCM traditions. Cook white rice with plenty of water until it forms a thick, creamy porridge. Add thinly sliced ginger (warming, helps clear residual Phlegm) and a pinch of salt. For Lung support, add sliced pear or a few goji berries.
  • Bone broth with astragalus — Astragalus (Huang Qi, 黄芪) is a traditional herb used in TCM for Qi deficiency, particularly Lung Qi deficiency. Simmer chicken or beef bones with astragalus slices, ginger, and dates for 4-6 hours. Strain and drink as a warm broth throughout the day.
  • Steamed pears with rock sugar — In TCM theory, pears are traditionally used to moisten the Lungs and clear residual Heat. Core a pear, fill with a few goji berries and rock sugar, and steam until soft. This is a gentle, nourishing Lung support.
  • Millet porridge or congee — Millet is traditionally considered a Spleen-tonifying grain that's easier to digest than wheat or oats. Cook with water or bone broth for a warming breakfast.
  • Avoid cold, raw, and dampening foods — In TCM theory, cold foods require additional Spleen Qi to warm up and digest. Raw salads, smoothies, dairy, and excessive sweets can aggravate Dampness and slow recovery. Save these for when your energy is more stable.

2. Prioritize the Breath

Rebuilding Lung Qi is foundational to post-COVID recovery. This doesn't mean pushing your lungs with intense cardio — it means gently retraining the respiratory system:

  • Diaphragmatic breathing (3-5 minutes, 3x daily) — Lie on your back with one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Inhale slowly through your nose, allowing your belly to rise, and exhale through pursed lips for twice as long. This strengthens the diaphragm and helps clear residual Phlegm.
  • Alternate nostril breathing — In TCM theory, this practice balances Lung Qi and clears the nasal passages. Close your right nostril, inhale through the left, close both, hold briefly, then exhale through the right. Alternate for 3-5 rounds.
  • Gentle Qi Gong — "Lion shakes its mane" — Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent. On an inhale, raise your arms overhead. On the exhale, bend forward from the hips, letting your arms hang loose, and gently shake your hands and shoulders. This movement is traditionally used to clear Lung channel stagnation and release tension from the upper back.
  • Postural support — Sitting hunched forward compresses the rib cage and limits the diaphragm's range. Every hour, try to sit upright, roll your shoulders back, and take 3 deep breaths. This simple adjustment can measurably improve comfort in the upper back and breathing mechanics.

3. Respect the Pacing Principle

One of the most critical — and most difficult — aspects of post-COVID recovery is pacing. In TCM theory, when Qi is depleted, activity consumes more energy than it produces. Pushing through exercise or work creates a deficit that takes days to recover from.

  • The 70% rule — Stop before you're exhausted. If a walk feels good at 10 minutes but tiring at 15, stop at 10. The goal is activity that leaves you feeling the same or slightly better the next day — not worse.
  • Listen for PEM (Post-Exertional Malaise) — If you feel significantly worse 12-48 hours after an activity, that activity was too much. This is a hallmark of post-viral fatigue and requires genuine pacing — not willpower.
  • Start with gentle walking — 5-10 minutes of slow walking is often the safest starting point. Build by 1-2 minutes per week, not per day. The TCM principle is: small, consistent steps that don't deplete reserves.

4. Support Kidney Qi With Rest Timing

In TCM theory, the Kidney system is most active during rest — particularly sleep between 11 PM and 3 AM, when the body's deepest restoration occurs. For post-COVID Kidney depletion:

  • Prioritize early sleep — Aim to be in bed by 10:30 PM. The hours before midnight are traditionally considered the most restorative for rebuilding Kidney Jing.
  • Use naps strategically — A short nap (15-30 minutes) between 1-2 PM can support recovery without disrupting nighttime sleep. This aligns with the body's natural circadian energy dip and, in TCM theory, supports the Spleen's afternoon energy cycle.
  • Warm your lower back before bed — The Kidneys are located in the lower back. Applying a warm compress, hot water bottle, or taking a warm foot bath before bed can help draw Qi downward and promote Kidney restoration.

5. Consider Traditional Acupressure Points

Acupressure is a gentle, self-administered practice from TCM that may support recovery. Here are points traditionally used for post-illness fatigue:

  • ST36 (Zusanli, below the knee) — Located four finger-widths below the kneecap, one finger-width toward the outer edge of the shin. Gentle pressure here is traditionally used to strengthen Spleen Qi and support overall energy.
  • CV6 (Qihai, below the navel) — One and a half finger-widths below the navel. Gentle palm pressure in a clockwise motion is traditionally used to support the body's Qi foundation.
  • K3 (Taixi, behind the ankle) — Located in the depression between the inner ankle bone and the Achilles tendon. Gentle pressure here is traditionally used to support Kidney Qi and Shen (spirit) during recovery.
  • LU9 (Taiyuan, at the wrist crease) — Located at the wrist crease on the thumb side, in the depression just below the base of the thumb. Gentle pressure here is traditionally used to support Lung Qi.

What Research Says About TCM and Post-COVID Recovery

As post-COVID fatigue has become a global health concern, researchers have begun studying traditional approaches:

  • A 2022 systematic review in Frontiers in Medicine examined TCM approaches for COVID-19 recovery and found that certain herbal formulas — particularly those containing Astragalus (Huang Qi), Licorice (Gan Cao), and Schisandra (Wu Wei Zi) — were associated with improvements in fatigue and respiratory symptoms in observational studies.
  • A 2021 pilot study in EBCAM found that acupuncture and herbal medicine were associated with reduced fatigue severity in post-COVID patients compared to usual care alone, though the study was small and non-randomized.
  • Research on the gut-lung axis has found parallels between the TCM concept of "Spleen-Lung connection" and modern understanding of how the gut microbiome influences respiratory immune function — suggesting that digestive health directly impacts respiratory recovery.
  • A 2023 narrative review in Integrative Medicine Research noted that several TCM herbal preparations used traditionally for Qi deficiency show immunological effects that may be relevant to post-COVID recovery, including modulation of inflammatory cytokines and support of mitochondrial function.

This research is promising but preliminary. TCM approaches should be considered complementary to — not a replacement for — medical care and rehabilitation guidance from your healthcare provider.

Practical Takeaways

If you're recovering from COVID and struggling with persistent fatigue, here are some steps to explore with your healthcare provider:

  1. Identify your dominant pattern. Use the self-assessment table above. Are you primarily breathless (Lung), heavy and foggy (Spleen/Dampness), profoundly drained (Kidney), or experiencing fixed pains (Blood Stasis)? Your starting point depends on your answer.
  2. Start with food and breath. Congee with ginger, warm broth, and gentle breathing exercises are the safest and most accessible first steps. These support both Lung and Spleen simultaneously.
  3. Pace, don't push. The hardest thing to accept about post-COVID fatigue is that resting is recovery. Every time you push through to exhaustion, you set yourself back. The TCM approach is to build capacity slowly, respecting your current limits.
  4. Track your energy patterns. Notice when your fatigue peaks and what makes it better or worse. This information is valuable for any practitioner you work with.
  5. Rule out other causes. If fatigue persists beyond 6-8 weeks after your acute infection, consult a medical doctor to rule out other post-COVID complications such as cardiac issues, blood clotting disorders, or autoimmune conditions.
  6. Consider working with a qualified practitioner. A licensed TCM practitioner can identify your specific pattern combination and develop a personalized approach using acupuncture, herbal support, and lifestyle guidance tailored to post-COVID recovery.

When to See a Doctor

⚠️ While post-COVID fatigue is common, certain symptoms require immediate medical attention. Seek care if you experience: chest pain or pressure, severe shortness of breath that's new or worsening, high fever that returns after improving, confusion or difficulty concentrating (especially if sudden), or new weakness on one side of the body. Also consult your doctor if fatigue is severe enough that you cannot perform basic daily activities, if you experience heart palpitations or dizziness upon standing, or if you develop new pain or swelling in your legs (which could indicate a blood clot). TCM is a complementary perspective — it does not replace medical diagnosis or treatment.
